Kirchhoff’s Laws

Introduction

In an electrical circuit, it is important to determine the current in each wire. The relation between resistance, voltage and current in a circuit is described by Ohm’s law and Kirchhoff’s laws.

When modelling a circuit mathematically, we assign a direction to each wire. In the circuit on Figure 1, it is for instance marked that the current \(I_3\) runs from left to right. If the system of equations later give that \(I_3\) is negative, this simply means that the current actually flows in the opposite direction.

Kirchhoff’s current law states that the sum of all currents in a node is zero (taking the current direction into account). Alternatively, one may phrase it as the sum of currents entering a node must equal the sum of current leaving the node. The other of Kirchhoff’s laws – the voltage law – states that within every loop of the circuit, the sum of voltages must be zero. Again, one must remember the assumed current directions.

Exercise 3

Finally, we explore how smaller parts of a circuit alter the current running through them. More precisely, we look at series circuits and shunt circuits. The voltage \(v\) and current \(i\) are represented by a vector \(\left[ \begin{array}{c} v\\ i \end{array} \right]\), and if this is sent through one of the two circuits, the current on the other side can be described by a linear transformation. For a series circuit with resistance \(R\), the matrix representation for the transformation is \[A_R=\left[ \begin{array}{rr} 1 & -R\\ 0 & 1 \end{array} \right],\] and for a shunt circuit with resistance \(R\), the transformation is \[B_R=\left[ \begin{array}{rr} 1 & 0\\ -\frac{1}{R} & 1 \end{array} \right].\]

  1. Show that when an electric current is sent through a shunt circuit with resistance \(R_1\) and afterwards through a series circuit with resistance \(R_2\), then the corresponding linear transformation has matrix representation \[\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3} A_{R_2}B_{R_1}= \left[ \begin{array}{rr} 1+\frac{R_2}{R_1} & -R_2\\ -\frac{1}{R_1} & 1 \end{array} \right].\] Then determine the resulting current and voltage if \(\left[ \begin{array}{c} v\\ i \end{array} \right]\) is sent through the circuit.

  2. Let \(R_1=3\) and \(R_2=4\), and define the matrices \[P=\left[ \begin{array}{rr} 2 & -6\\ 1 & 1 \end{array} \right] \quad\text{and}\quad D=\left[ \begin{array}{rr} \frac{1}{3} & 0\\ 0 & 3 \end{array} \right].\]
